Progress Review

Achieving My Goals

In terms of whether or not I achieved the goals I set for myself at the beginning of the term, I would say that I made significant progress with respect to each of them, while also recognizing that there is more I can do in my second work term with Brock Solutions. I would also note that some of these goals evolved as my role at Brock changed and as I became more familiar with both my team and my responsibilities.

Visual Studio

I improved my ability to navigate the codebase, trace features and bugs through the mobile client and API, and begin debugging with much more confidence than I had at the beginning of the term.

SQL Server Management Studio

I became better at finding and querying the right tables when working on data-related bugs and features, which helped me understand where issues existed and how data was being stored and retrieved.

SharePoint/TFS

I became more effective at communicating my work through PRs, comments, and workflow tracking, and more comfortable participating in discussions about expected behaviour and implementation decisions.

Reflection by Goal

  1. For goal #1, relating to seeking clarification about expected behaviour, I would say that I made significant progress during the work term. Early in the term, when I was not completely certain how something should work, I would often make an assumption based on the work item and other context clues and move too quickly toward a solution. The problem was that my solution would sometimes differ from what was actually expected. During the term, I learned to spend more time investigating expected behaviour, fully understanding the problem and why it was occurring, asking questions when necessary, and then making a fix based on that information.
  2. For goal #2, relating to time management and time-boxing, I would also say that I made progress, though there is still more I can do in this area. I became better at not working indefinitely on an issue without having a clear direction or a rough time box for finishing it. However, when I sought help, it was usually not because of an explicit time box, but rather because I had the sense that I was no longer making meaningful progress. In my next work term, I want to do a better job of setting explicit time windows while still giving myself some flexibility to adjust them. Another skill I want to improve is asking questions about potential blockers before I encounter them. I find that when I am assigned work and do not yet know exactly how to approach it, I sometimes struggle to articulate areas of concern until I actually run into them.
  3. My third goal, relating to recording pain points and issues, is one on which I was able to make solid progress, thanks to my team members and supervisor. To achieve this goal, I made sure to be transparent during our daily standup meetings by communicating my progress, the issues I was facing, and asking for assistance when needed. During retrospectives, I also shared pain points and recurring patterns or issues that I noticed were slowing me down. My supervisor encouraged my co-workers and me to share any pain points, blockers, or issues we encountered during our work each week. I took advantage of that opportunity to explain why I sometimes felt slower than I expected and to reflect on what I could do to reduce the impact of those issues.
  4. For goal #4, relating to testing, I would say that I made progress, but there is still more I want to do. When working on bug fixes, I became better at testing with several different pieces of data and through several different flows. When working on features, I became more proactive about writing unit tests and looking for edge cases that might break my code. In terms of further improvement, I want to do a better job of using TDD (test-driven development) and red-green-refactor cycles, where I begin by writing the minimum failing test, then write the minimum code needed to make it pass, and then refactor. I also want to do a better job of testing my changes against the database. Beyond following the flow through the mobile app and Visual Studio, I want to ensure that the states of the data in the database are accurate.
  5. For goal #5, relating to completing tasks in order of priority, I am very happy with the progress I made and with where I am currently at with this goal. I have become more involved in conversations about the priority of different work items, such as which bugs should be addressed first and whether a reopened issue should take precedence over something already in progress. When I am unsure which item should be prioritized, I have no problem asking my supervisor or other team members for clarification.
